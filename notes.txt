1. CONVERSION
- After emancipation, conversion was a CHOICE (not forced like the Crusades or Inquisition).
- It was social/political, not religious. At least 250,000 Jews converted. Disraeli became British PM.

HEINRICH HEINE (p. 13): German Jewish poet. Baptized Lutheran in 1825 to get a doctorate and a government/university job. It didn't work; moved to Paris. Kept his Jewishness privately.
  - "The baptismal certificate is the ticket of admission to European culture." (KEY)
  - "From the nature of my thinking you can deduce that baptism is a matter of indifference to me."
  - "For me perhaps its significance will be that I can better devote myself to championing the rights of my unfortunate brethren."
  - "I hold it beneath my dignity and a stain on my honor to undergo conversion in order to obtain a position in Prussia."
  - "I'll turn Catholic yet for spite, and hang myself."
  - "I make no secret of my Judaism, to which I have not returned, because I never left it."
  - "Where they burn books, they ultimately burn people."

ABRAHAM MENDELSSOHN (pp. 11-12): son of Moses Mendelssohn (father of the Haskalah). Letter to daughter Fanny on why he raised her Christian: Judaism 'had its day', Christianity is the creed of the most civilized people.
  - "We have educated you and your brothers and sister in the Christian faith, because it is the creed of most civilized people." (KEY)
  - "Some thousands of years ago the Jewish form was the reigning one, then the heathen form, and now it is the Christian." (KEY)
  - "The outward form of religion your teacher has given you is historical, and changeable like all human ordinances."
  - "I know it, I believe it, I live in this faith, and this is my religion."
  - "By pronouncing your confession of faith you have fulfilled the claims of society on you, and obtained the name of a Christian."
  - "Because it is the religious form acceptable to the majority of civilized human beings."

HEINE vs MENDELSSOHN: Heine = get ahead, avoid discrimination, baptism is a ticket. Mendelssohn = Christianity is today's religion of civilized people. Both social/political, not religious.

2. REFORM: BEGINNINGS (pp. 14-15)
- Goal of early Reform: NOT to destroy Judaism. Make it understandable and attractive so Jews stop converting out.
ISRAEL JACOBSON'S TEMPLE (Seesen, 1810): not a rabbi. Christians attended, orchestra, choir, organ, hymns in German and Hebrew. Goal: religious education.
  - "What I had in mind when I first thought about building this temple was your religious education." (KEY)
  - "Be it far from me that I should have any secret intention to undermine the pillars of your faith." (KEY)
  - "On all sides, enlightenment opens up new areas for development. Why should we alone remain behind?" (KEY)
  - "Who would dare to deny that our service is sickly because of many useless things, that in part it has degenerated into a thoughtless recitation of prayers and formulas."
  - "If I do seek here first some rapprochement between you and our Christian neighbors, I would ask more for your gratitude and honest help than for your criticism."
HAMBURG TEMPLE (constitution 1817): founded by 66 laymen. German sermon, choir with organ, confirmation for boys AND girls. Worship was neglected because people no longer knew Hebrew.
  - "Since public worship has for some time been neglected by so many, because of the ever decreasing knowledge of the language in which alone it has until now been conducted..." (KEY)
  - "Specifically, there shall be introduced at such services a German sermon, and choral singing to the accompaniment of an organ."
  - "The children of both sexes, after having received adequate schooling in the teachings of the faith, shall be accepted as confirmants of the Mosaic religion."
  - "Convinced of the necessity to restore public worship to its deserving dignity and importance."
  - "By adopting the designation traditionally reserved for the fallen Temple of Jerusalem, the Reformers symbolically relinquished the hope of Israel's restoration and declared Hamburg their Jerusalem."
WHY 'TEMPLE'? (1) Sounds less foreign / less Jewish than synagogue. (2) Name of the destroyed Jerusalem Temple: they gave up hope of returning to Zion. Hamburg = their Jerusalem. 'We're here to stay.'

3. REFORM: MAIN ISSUES (pp. 17-18, 22-23)
- Issues: prayer language (Hebrew vs vernacular), head covering, kashrut, Sabbath Saturday vs Sunday.
- Frankfurt conference (1845): is Hebrew legally needed for prayer?
ZECHARIAS FRANKEL: team Hebrew. Not legally required but CONSERVE it, it's sacred to the people, prevents a schism. Positive-historical Judaism. Founder of Conservative Judaism.
  - "Now it is necessary to conserve the things which are truly sacred to the entire people, to prevent any schism in Israel." (KEY)
  - "In order to understand Judaism in the present one must look back and investigate its past."
  - "The positive forms of Judaism are deeply rooted within its innermost being and must not be discarded coldly and heartlessly."
  - "The Zeitgeist is as fickle as the times. Besides, it is cold."
  - "Rather than creating new parties, we must make peace between the existing ones."
  - "It is the pride of Judaism that no person, and no social class, may presume authority, but that every decision must evolve organically from principles."
ABRAHAM GEIGER: team German. German prayer strikes a deeper chord (you understand it). Judaism is a religion, NOT a nation. Refused to move Shabbat to Sunday or abolish circumcision.
  - "A German prayer strikes a deeper chord than a Hebrew prayer." (KEY)
  - "No member of this conference would wish to link Judaism to a particular nation." (KEY)
  - "Consequently, the national aspect of Israel must recede into the background." (KEY)
  - "Anyone who imagines Judaism to be walking on the crutches of a language deeply offends it."
  - "The Hebrew language has ceased to be alive for the people."
  - "Wholly faded from our consciousness is the belief in the restoration of a Jewish State in Palestine."
  - "The look into the future should arouse the happy hope in the unification of all mankind, in truth, in justice, and in peace."
  - "The separation between Israel and the other peoples, which existed at one time, has no right to be expressed in prayer."
GEIGER'S PRAYER BOOK PREFACE, 3 main issues: (1) no Israel/Zion, (2) no Messiah, rebuilt Temple, ingathering of exiles, (3) universalism / openness to other religions.

4. REFORM: EVOLUTION (pp. 24-28)
PITTSBURGH PLATFORM (1885), Classical Reform. Called by Kaufmann Kohler, chaired by Isaac M. Wise, adopted by the CCAR.
  - "We consider ourselves no longer a nation, but a religious community, and therefore expect neither a return to Palestine, nor a sacrificial worship under the sons of Aaron." (KEY)
  - "All such Mosaic and rabbinical laws as regulate diet, priestly purity, and dress ... fail to impress the modern Jew with a spirit of priestly holiness."
  - "Today we accept as binding only the moral laws, and maintain only such ceremonies as elevate and sanctify our lives."
  - "We recognize in Judaism a progressive religion, ever striving to be in accord with the postulates of reason."
  - "We recognize in every religion an attempt to grasp the Infinite."
  - "We reject as ideas not rooted in Judaism the beliefs both in bodily resurrection and in Gehenna and Eden."
COLUMBUS PLATFORM (1937), CCAR. Why it changed: Nazi Nuremberg Laws (1935), assimilation failed (even converts not accepted), Zionism succeeding.
  - "Judaism is the soul of which Israel is the body." (KEY)
  - "We affirm the obligation of all Jewry to aid in its upbuilding as a Jewish homeland." (KEY)
  - "The use of Hebrew, together with the vernacular, in our worship and instruction."
  - "The preservation of the Sabbath, festivals and Holy Days, the retention and development of such customs, symbols and ceremonies as possess inspirational value."
  - "Judaism is the historical religious experience of the Jewish people."
  - "It presents them not as a fixed creed but as a guide for the progressive elements of Jewry."
  - "Not only a haven of refuge for the oppressed but also a center of Jewish culture and spiritual life."
EVOLUTION SUMMARY: Pittsburgh = not a nation, no return to Palestine, only moral laws binding, drop diet/dress laws. Columbus = Jewish people again ('soul of which Israel is the body'), help build a Jewish homeland, Hebrew together with the vernacular, keep Sabbath/festivals/inspiring customs. Reform today is NOT anti-Israel.

TIMELINE (order matters, dates don't): 1810 Israel Jacobson opens his little "Temple" in Seesen -> 1817-18 Hamburg Temple constitution and dedication -> 1822-25 Mendelssohn family and Heine convert -> 1845 Frankfurt conference: Frankel vs. Geiger on Hebrew -> 1845+ Geiger's new prayer book: national aspect recedes -> 1885 Pittsburgh Platform: Classical Reform -> 1935 Nazi Nuremberg Laws strip German Jews of citizenship -> 1937 Columbus Platform: peoplehood, homeland, Hebrew return

PRACTICE SHORT ANSWERS (with model answers)
1. Compare Heine's and Mendelssohn's reasons for converting.
   Model: Heine converted to get ahead in society; he saw the baptismal certificate as a ticket of admission to European culture and a job. Mendelssohn raised his children Christian because he saw Christianity as the religion of the most civilized people today, while Judaism had its day. Both were social or political reasons, not religious ones.
2. Explain the quote: "The baptismal certificate is the ticket of admission to European culture." Who said it and what does it mean?
   Model: Heinrich Heine said it. He meant that for a Jew to be fully accepted in European society, get a career and avoid discrimination, he had to be baptized; it was a social move, not a religious belief.
3. How was conversion after emancipation different from conversion in the Middle Ages?
   Model: In the Middle Ages (Crusades, Inquisition) Jews were forced to convert or be killed. After emancipation conversion was a free choice, and with religion playing a smaller role it became a secular, social and political act to be accepted in society.
4. What was the original intent of the Reform movement according to Jacobson and the Hamburg Temple?
   Model: Jacobson said his temple was for religious education, not to undermine the pillars of the faith. The Hamburg Temple said public worship was neglected because people no longer knew Hebrew, so they made it attractive and understandable (German sermon, organ, choir) to keep Jews from leaving or converting.
5. Give two reasons the Reformers called their house of worship a "temple".
   Model: First, "temple" sounded less foreign and less Jewish than synagogue, like the places of worship of other religions. Second, by using the name of the destroyed Temple in Jerusalem, they gave up hope of returning to Zion and declared Hamburg their Jerusalem: we are here to stay.
6. List two changes the Hamburg Temple brought into Jewish worship and explain why.
   Model: It added a German sermon and choir singing with an organ, and confirmation for both boys and girls. The goal was to make worship dignified, understandable and attractive, like their Christian neighbours, because people no longer understood Hebrew and stopped coming.
7. Explain the debate between Frankel and Geiger at the Frankfurt conference.
   Model: They argued whether Hebrew is needed in prayer. Frankel said it is not legally required but must be conserved because it is sacred to the whole people and prevents a schism. Geiger said a German prayer strikes a deeper chord because people understand it, and that Judaism is a religion, not a nation tied to one language.
8. Explain Geiger's line: "the national aspect of Israel must recede into the background."
   Model: In the preface to his new prayer book, Geiger said Judaism is only a religion, not a nation. So prayers about returning to Zion, a Jewish state in Palestine, rebuilding the Temple and the ingathering of exiles were removed, and prayers focused on hope for all mankind instead.
9. What were the three main issues in Geiger's preface to the Frankfurt prayer book?
   Model: One: the connection to Israel and Zion is removed from prayer. Two: no more praying for the Messiah, rebuilding the Temple or the ingathering of exiles. Three: universalism, being open to other religions and hoping for the unification of all mankind instead of stressing Jewish separation.
10. Describe the Pittsburgh Platform (1885). What did Classical Reform believe?
   Model: It said Jews are no longer a nation but a religious community, so no return to Palestine. Only the moral laws are binding, and laws about diet, priestly purity and dress (like kashrut) don't fit modern life. Judaism is a progressive religion in accord with reason.
11. Why did the Reform movement change its views between 1885 and 1937?
   Model: By 1937 the Nazis' Nuremberg Laws (1935) showed assimilation had failed: Jews were not accepted as Germans even if they converted, and Hitler went after them whether religion or nation. Zionism in Palestine was succeeding, so Reform brought back Jewish peoplehood and support for a homeland.
12. Compare the Pittsburgh and Columbus Platforms on Israel and on Hebrew.
   Model: Pittsburgh (1885) said Jews are not a nation and expect no return to Palestine, and it drops rituals. Columbus (1937) says Judaism is the soul of which Israel is the body, asks all Jews to help build a Jewish homeland, and brings back Hebrew together with the vernacular.
13. Is it right to say Reform Judaism is against Israel? Explain using the evolution of Reform.
   Model: No. Early Reform (Geiger, Pittsburgh 1885) removed Zion and said Jews are not a nation. But the Columbus Platform (1937) changed this and called on all Jews to help build a Jewish homeland, and Reform today supports Israel.
